Taniti is a fictional tropical island used as the brief for a Western Governors
University user-experience project. The assignment was to design the tourism
site, not build it, so there is no code repository. The work lives in the
research, structure, wireframes and interactive mockup.

## Starting with the trip, not the homepage

The project began with a traveler persona and a content inventory. The persona
favored hotels, local food, beaches, sightseeing and occasional outdoor
activities, while still needing practical answers about transport, safety and
holiday closures. That made the site's job larger than selling scenery: it had
to support discovery, planning and booking without hiding basic travel facts.

I turned that into a sitemap with five main paths - the island, experiences,
hotels, planning and packages - and detail pages that converge on booking. A
separate flow diagram checked that the calls to action across the screens
actually led somewhere useful.

## Building the prototype

The visual system uses ocean blue, a small warm-orange accent and large,
high-contrast interaction targets. Destination photography does the emotional
work while the page structure stays task-oriented: explore the island, compare
an experience or place to stay, then move toward a quote or booking request.

Nine desktop screens were assembled into an InVision prototype: home, island
overview, experiences, experience detail, lodging, lodging detail, packages,
package detail and booking. Supporting trip-planning content and a repeated
four-step journey strip keep the visitor oriented between those routes.

## Testing changed the labels

Because this was completed in 2020, testing was remote. Four participants worked
through a structured Google Forms survey against the prototype. The small sample
was enough to expose a specific information-architecture problem: attractions
and booking were easy to find, but half of the participants could not identify
where lodging lived.

The original header used the short label “Stay.” Feedback described it as vague
and asked for wording closer to “book your stay,” so the final navigation names
the destination directly as **Hotels**. Testing also showed that the booking
deadline on a package detail page was technically present but visually easy to
miss; the revision promotes it next to availability instead of leaving it in the
body copy.

## What this artifact is - and is not

This is a high-fidelity design prototype, not a production travel site. Some
detail screens intentionally retain placeholder imagery and copy, and an early
mobile exploration was removed after the assignment requirements were clarified.
The source photography, icons and placeholder services are recorded in the
original project citations; the interface design, information architecture,
prototype and testing work are mine.
